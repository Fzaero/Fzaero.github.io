About Me

I recently completed my PhD in Electrical Engineering at Chalmers University of Technology, supervised by Yasemin Bekiroğlu. I am currently a Project Assistant at Chalmers University of Technology.

During my PhD, my research focused on data-efficient representation learning for robotic grasping and manipulation. In particular, I developed implicit representations for transferring grasps across objects, neural field representations for visual learning from demonstration, and dynamical systems for generating reactive motions, including complex trajectories with intersections.

Before joining Chalmers, I completed my MSc in Computer Engineering at Boğaziçi University, where I worked as a research assistant under the supervision of Emre Uğur. I was among the first members of the Cognition, Learning, and Robotics (CoLoRs) research group and contributed to the EU Horizon 2020 Robots Understanding Their Actions by Imagining Their Effects (IMAGINE) project, investigating how robots can learn to predict the effects of their actions on the environment.

My research interests lie in robot learning, learning from demonstration, spatial representations, and reactive manipulation. I am particularly interested in how robots can learn structured representations of their environments, including physical dynamics and task-relevant relationships, to acquire, adapt, and reuse manipulation skills. I am also interested in leveraging pretrained visual and multimodal representations to improve generalization across objects, tasks, and environments.
